Inspiration isn't a place you browse. It's where Aurora shows its work.

A brief that asks for an "inspiration page" is asking for a feed. Aurora can't ship a feed — it would argue against the product it lives in. So this isn't a page of things you might want. It's your day, with the open space made valuable.

The tension

The brief's own words point away from a feed

"Prompting is for losers… The real breakthrough will be the elimination of the question altogether." Alex Macdonald, Aurora

And from the co-founder's launch essay: a product that "pulls you toward real experiences rather than keeping you stuck comparing options on a screen." A grid of beautiful cards is a screen you compare options on.

The fix isn't a prettier feed. It's changing what the surface is. Chat serves "I know what I want." Inspiration serves "I didn't know to ask" — and its real job is to make the autopilot legible, so a member can trust something they never requested.

Structure

The unit of the interface is the gap

Every experience members describe as magic has the same shape: it was fitted into a hole in a day that already existed. Recovery between meetings. A track session between conference sessions. A reroute to catch an old friend. None of it was a wishlist. All of it was interstitial.

So the page is the day. Committed time is printed grey and the hairline rules hold it together. Where the day opens, the rule breaks and light fills the break — and Aurora writes into it. The gaps are finite, so this surface cannot become infinite. It ends where your day ends.

Screens to reserve one thing. Everyone else ends on a green tick after a price breakdown, a payment sheet and a page of terms.

So confirming had to be worth the gesture. You hold, rather than tap — and the light charges while you do. Let go early and it glides back with nothing spent. Hold it through and the card doesn't disappear and get replaced: the block morphs. Its height glides while the reasoning dissolves into the work Aurora is doing, struck as points down the beam. Then the light withdraws, the rule closes across the break, and the entry settles into your day.

Nothing here jumps, and nothing bounces. Every move is one continuous shape changing into another, which is what makes it read as execution rather than confirmation.

Rules

What it holds itself to

  • Two or three items. Never a catalogue. Volume is the tell of a system that hasn't decided.
  • The reason before the thing. Every card opens with why this, why now, why you.
  • Real windows, not urgency theatre. A counter released two seats; you're in London until Sunday.
  • One gesture to done. A form here would make this a marketplace.
  • Restraint made visible. Aurora leaves gaps open on purpose. A host who fills every silence isn't attentive.
Craft

No photography anywhere. That's the risk, and it's the argument

Every reference in this category is a photo-driven catalogue. Images exist to help you choose between options; Aurora has already chosen, so imagery would reintroduce exactly the comparison this surface removes.

What's left has to carry it: an old-style serif for anything Aurora says in its own voice, a humanist sans for the machinery, times set with points rather than colons, tabular figures, hairlines instead of shadows, and no monospace anywhere. Motion is a single decelerating curve used everywhere, never a spring — this product should feel unhurried, not playful. The aurora is never a text colour — it exists only as light in open time, drifting slowly, brightening when you attend to it, charging when you commit.

Retention

Why this earns a daily open with no feed mechanics

No infinite scroll, no streaks, no badges, no trending. The return isn't habit, it's consequence. The day changes overnight — you land somewhere, a counter opens, sleep debt compounds — so the surface is genuinely different each morning and the windows close whether you look or not. You open it for the same reason you check the weather.

The hard one

Some days there's nothing worth moving

A feed can never be empty; the business model forbids it. Aurora's can — and should. The quiet day is the strongest available proof that the system has taste, so it's built as a first-class screen rather than an edge case, and it still tells you what Aurora is holding.

Scope

Read as the in-app discovery surface of the existing mobile product, not a marketing page. Multi-device and multiplayer are acknowledged roadmap and deliberately out of scope. Aurora's real product screens weren't publicly available, so the visual language is drawn from the brand's own ground — black, and light arriving in it.
